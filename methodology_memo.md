## 1. Purpose and scope

Journal entry testing is conventionally performed on a judgemental or statistical sample — commonly twenty-five entries drawn from a population of tens of thousands. The remainder of the population goes untested, and the exception rate across it is unknown.

This project tests the **entire population** of 84,106 journal entries against eight procedures, and — unusually for portfolio work — scores its own detection rate against a known answer key.

The assertion being tested is the **occurrence and validity of recorded journal entries**: are these entries genuine, appropriately authorised, and recorded in the correct period?

Out of scope: completeness of the underlying transactions themselves, valuation of the balances, and any conclusion about the design or operating effectiveness of the client's controls.

## 2. Data

### Source

A synthetic general ledger generated by `01_generate_ledger.ipynb`. **No client data is used at any point.**

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `general_ledger.csv` | 168,212 line items across 84,106 journal entries |
| `trial_balance.csv` | 34 accounts with debit, credit and net balances |
| `answer_key.csv` | 124 deliberately planted anomalies with type and reason |

The generator uses a fixed random seed (240726). Anyone re-running the notebook produces a byte-identical ledger, which makes every figure in this memo independently verifiable.

### Why synthetic data

Real ledgers cannot be published, and a project built on one could not be verified by a reader. More importantly, a real ledger has no ground truth: you can report what a workflow found, but not what it missed.

Planting anomalies and recording them in an answer key converts detection from an assertion into a measurement.

### Structure

Each journal entry comprises exactly two line items — line 1 the debit, line 2 the credit — so the population balances by construction. Fields include posting date, effective date, account code and name, cost centre, user ID, entry type (manual or system), narration, amount, and approval status.

### Parameters applied

| Parameter | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Planning materiality | 2,500,000 | Drives the round-number test |
| Approval threshold | 500,000 | Entries above this require approval |
| Financial year | 1 Apr 2025 – 31 Mar 2026 | Indian FY 2025–26 |

## 3. Approach

The work follows a seven-step method carried over from statutory audit rather than from analytics practice. The distinction matters: a data scientist validates a model against a test set; a Chartered Accountant additionally asks whether the result ties to the trial balance, whether a reviewer can re-perform it, and whether it will still run next period.

1. **Scope the assertion, not the deliverable.** State the question before opening the data.
2. **Prove completeness before analysing anything.** Row counts, control totals, period coverage.
3. **Tie to the source of truth and document any difference.**
4. **Build the logic one movement at a time**, never netted, so each step is separately inspectable.
5. **Design the exception, not the report.** A defensible list with a stated threshold and written rationale.
6. **Build the audit trail as you go**, not afterwards.
7. **Re-run on the next period's data before calling it done.**

## 4. Procedures performed

### 4.1 Completeness (before any testing)

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Line items | 168,212 |
| Journal entries | 84,106 |
| Total debits | 30,523,085,595.19 |
| Total credits | 30,523,085,595.19 |
| Difference | 0.00 — balanced |
| Nulls in key fields | None |
| Trial balance reconciliation | 34 of 34 accounts agree; maximum difference 0.00 |

**Finding — period coverage.** Posting dates extend to 9 May 2026, beyond the financial year end. Investigation identified **36 line items across 18 entries** with effective dates in March 2026 and posting dates in April and May — year-end accruals recorded after the period closed.

This is normal practice, not an error. Its significance is procedural: **any analysis filtering this population on posting date alone would silently exclude eighteen genuine year-end entries**, and would produce a clean-looking result on an incomplete population. That is worse than no analysis, because it carries false confidence.

The population was therefore scoped on effective date, with posting date retained as a test attribute.

### 4.2 Standardisation

Line items were collapsed to entry level (84,106 rows) and five test attributes derived: day of week, holiday flag, posting lag in days, debit/credit account pairing, and numeric sequence.

### 4.3 The eight procedures

| Ref | Procedure | Rationale | Exceptions |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Round-number manual postings at or above materiality | Genuine transactions rarely settle on exact hundred-thousands | 14 |
| T2 | Manual entries posted on weekends or public holidays | Routine business does not require out-of-hours posting | 29 |
| T3 | Entries by users posting fewer than ten times in the year | An infrequent user posting a material entry warrants enquiry | 9 |
| T4 | Entries posted more than seven days after effective date | Back-dating around a period boundary affects cut-off | 18 |
| T5 | Manual amounts between 94% and 100% of the approval threshold | Repeated amounts just below a limit may indicate deliberate splitting | 445 |
| T6 | Debit/credit account pairings occurring fewer than five times | Unusual pairings each require an explanation | 8 |
| T7 | Narrations containing review-trigger words | Language such as "plug", "temporary" or "to be corrected" signals unresolved items | 26 |
| T8 | Gaps in the journal entry sequence | Missing entry IDs may indicate deletion or incomplete extraction | 9 |

Keywords used in T7: plug, difference, reversal, temporary, pending, adjustment, squaring, correction, as per instruction, to be corrected.

**Benford's Law was deliberately excluded.** It assumes naturally occurring numbers; a corporate ledger is full of negotiated values — contract prices, regulated tariffs, standard rates. On this population it flags a high proportion of legitimate entries and produces a list too large to follow up.

## 5. Threshold design

Thresholds were set deliberately and the reasoning recorded, on the principle that a threshold without a documented rationale is an opinion rather than a control.

**T5 required revision.** The procedure as first designed — any entry between 94% and 100% of the approval threshold — returned **2,231 exceptions** to identify 26 genuine items, a precision of 1.2%. Of those, 1,786 were system-generated batch postings that happened to fall in the range.

Four designs were compared:

| Design | Exceptions | Caught | Missed | Precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 94–100%, all entries | 2,231 | 26 | 0 | 1.2% |
| **94–100%, manual only** | **445** | **26** | **0** | **5.8%** |
| 98–100%, manual only | 130 | 7 | 19 | 5.4% |
| 90–100%, manual only | 720 | 26 | 0 | 3.6% |

Restricting to manual entries improved precision fivefold at no cost in detection. Narrowing the band instead reduced the list further but lost nineteen of the twenty-six genuine items — a materially worse trade.

**The band was never the problem; the entry type was.** Two thousand exceptions is a failed design, because a list nobody works through is not a control. Four hundred and forty-five with a documented cut-off is a working procedure.

**Risk ranking.** Entries triggering three or more procedures were ranked High, two Medium, one Low, on the basis that corroboration across independent tests is a stronger signal than any single flag.

## 6. Results

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Entries tested | 84,106 |
| Total exception instances | 568 |
| Unique entries flagged | 542 |
| Exception rate | 0.64% |
| Planted anomalies | 124 |
| Anomalies detected | 124 (100%) |
| Anomalies missed | 0 |

Twenty-six entries triggered more than one procedure. Cross-corroboration of this kind is the basis of the risk ranking, and is one of the principal advantages of running procedures in parallel over the full population rather than sequentially over a sample.

Detection is reported against planted anomalies only. Entries flagged that were not planted are not errors — they are the false positive rate inherent in each threshold, and are reported as such rather than tuned away.

## 7. Implementation and sequence

The suite was built three times, deliberately.

### Stage 1 — Python (pandas)

**Purpose:** establish independently verified target figures before building in any visual tool.

A workflow's correctness cannot be judged by inspecting it; it can only be judged against a number already trusted. This stage exists to produce those numbers, and mirrors the audit habit of re-performing a calculation independently before accepting it.

**Output:** `01_generate_ledger.ipynb`, `02_reconnaissance.ipynb`.

### Stage 2 — Excel Power Query and Power Pivot

**Purpose:** implement the suite in the tool every finance function already has.

Eight test procedures built as Reference queries off a common staging query, appended and scored. Results loaded to the Power Pivot data model as a star schema, with DAX measures for exception rate, detection rate, precision and exceptions per thousand entries.

**Output:** `journal_entry_testing.xlsx`, M queries exported individually as text for review.

### Stage 3 — Alteryx Designer

**Purpose:** reviewability and productionisation.

The same eight procedures as parallel branches on a single canvas, organised into five containers — completeness, standardisation, testing, scoring, output. Packaged as an Analytic App with an input form so a non-technical reviewer can run the suite against their own extract with their own materiality and approval parameters.

**Output:** `.yxzp` workflow package, `.yxwz` analytic app, canvas screenshots, Excel exception workbook.

### Tool comparison

Excel was **not** a constraint at this scale. Power Query processed 168,212 rows without difficulty, is fully repeatable on refresh, and its Applied Steps pane constitutes a serviceable audit trail.

What Alteryx provided was different in kind:

- **Reviewability.** Eight parallel procedures visible simultaneously on one canvas, legible to a reviewer who does not write code. In Power Query the branch structure is inferred from a list of queries rather than seen.
- **Productionisation.** An interface layer allowing a non-technical user to run the suite on their own data with their own parameters.

Those, rather than raw processing capability, are why professional audit analytics functions standardise on tools of this type.

One procedure differed genuinely between implementations. **T8 (sequence gaps)** uses Alteryx's Multi-Row Formula to compare each row against the preceding one; Power Query has no equivalent, so the same result is obtained by generating the complete expected sequence and subtracting the observed values. Identical output, different conceptual model.

## 8. Limitations

- **Synthetic data.** Real ledgers contain messier narrations, inconsistent user conventions, and multi-line entries. Detection rates on real data would be lower.
- **Two-line entries only.** Real journals include compound entries with many lines. The account-pairing procedure would require redesign for those.
- **Detection measured against planted anomalies only.** The procedures may fail to detect patterns not represented in the answer key.
- **No control testing.** This work addresses recorded entries, not the design or operating effectiveness of the controls over them.
- **Thresholds are dataset-specific.** Materiality, the approval limit and the rare-user cut-off would each be set from engagement circumstances.
- **Analytics extends coverage; it does not replace professional scepticism.** Every exception raised here is an item requiring enquiry, not a conclusion.

1. Run `01_generate_ledger.ipynb` — regenerates the identical ledger from seed 240726.
2. Run `02_reconnaissance.ipynb` — reproduces every figure in this memo.

All four routes produce the same 124 detections from the same 84,106 entries.
